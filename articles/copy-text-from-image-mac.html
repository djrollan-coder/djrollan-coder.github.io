How to copy text from an image, video or PDF on a Mac

By Roman MotornyiNovember 20, 20263 min read

Text inside a picture, a paused video, a scanned PDF or an app that won't let you select anything is still just text. Here is how to get it into your clipboard on a Mac, from the built-in tools to a single shortcut that works everywhere.

1. Live Text — built into macOS

Since macOS Monterey, a Mac can recognise text in images by itself. Apple calls it Live Text, and it works in apps that support it: Preview, Photos, Quick Look and Safari.

  1. Open the image in Preview or Photos.
  2. Move the pointer over the text — it turns into a text cursor.
  3. Drag to select the words you need and press ⌘C.

On macOS Ventura and later, Live Text also works on a paused frame of a video in QuickTime Player and Safari.

The catch: it only works in those apps. A video call, a game, a remote desktop window, a design tool or an app that draws its own interface gives Live Text nothing to work with.

2. Take a screenshot, then use Live Text

When the text is in an app without Live Text, you can take a detour through a screenshot:

  1. Press ⌘⇧4 and drag over the area with the text.
  2. Click the thumbnail that appears in the corner of the screen, or open the saved file in Preview.
  3. Select the text in the screenshot and copy it.

It works, but it takes several steps and leaves a screenshot file on your desktop every time.

3. One shortcut, any app: Lingrab

Lingrab is a small menu bar app that does the whole thing in one move:

  1. Press ⌥⌘1.
  2. Drag over the area with the text.
  3. The text is already in your clipboard — paste it wherever you need.

It works on anything that is on screen: images, videos, PDFs, presentations, games and apps that don't let you select text. It reads photos too — signs, receipts and neat handwriting — and recognises 18 languages, including English, Russian, Ukrainian, German, French, Spanish, Chinese, Japanese and Korean.

Recognition runs entirely on your Mac. Nothing is uploaded, so it is safe for documents you would not want to send to an online OCR service.

Tips for better results

  • Zoom in first. Small or blurry text is recognised worse — enlarge the image or the page before you capture it.
  • Select only what you need. A tight selection skips menus, buttons and other text you would have to delete later.
  • Check numbers. In prices, codes and phone numbers, a 0 and an O or a 1 and an l can look alike. Glance over them after pasting.

Questions

Do I need an internet connection?

No. Both Live Text and Lingrab recognise text on the Mac itself.

Can it copy text from a video while it plays?

Live Text needs a paused frame. Lingrab captures whatever is on screen at the moment you finish the selection, so you can grab a subtitle or a slide without stopping anything.

Is Lingrab free?

Yes — copying text from the screen, fixing the keyboard layout, reading aloud and a clipboard history are free. Lingrab Pro, a one-time purchase, adds translation, voice settings, custom shortcuts and a longer history.
