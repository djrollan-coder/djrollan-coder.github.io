How to see your clipboard history on a Mac

By Roman MotornyiNovember 20, 20262 min readЧитать на русском

You copied a link, then a phone number, then a paragraph — and now you need the link again. On a Mac, the classic clipboard only remembers the last thing you copied. Here is what you can do about it.

See what is on the clipboard right now

In Finder, choose Edit → Show Clipboard. A small window shows the current contents. It is only ever one item: copying something new replaces it.

With Universal Clipboard, that one item also travels between your Mac, iPhone and iPad when they use the same Apple ID and have Handoff turned on — handy, but still a single item.

A clipboard history in Spotlight

macOS Tahoe 26 added a clipboard history to Spotlight. If your Mac runs it, open Spotlight and switch to its clipboard view to find recent items. On older versions of macOS there is no built-in history at all.

A history in the menu bar: Lingrab

Lingrab keeps everything you copy in its menu bar menu. Click an item and it is back on the clipboard, ready to paste. The free version remembers 20 items; Lingrab Pro remembers up to 500.

A clipboard history is only useful if it is safe, so Lingrab never saves:

  • passwords copied from password managers,
  • crypto wallet addresses and recovery phrases,
  • private keys and API keys.

The history stays on your Mac. Lingrab has no accounts and sends nothing anywhere.

Why it matters what a history skips

Password managers mark the items they put on the clipboard as concealed, so that well-behaved apps do not store them. A history that ignores the mark keeps your passwords in plain view in a menu. When you pick a clipboard manager, check that it respects concealed items — and that it filters secrets you copy by hand, such as seed phrases and API keys.

Questions

Does the history survive a restart?

The system clipboard does not: it is emptied when you log out or restart. A clipboard manager keeps its own history.

Can I copy text that isn't selectable into the history?

Yes. Text you grab from the screen with Lingrab's ⌥⌘1 goes to the clipboard and into the history like anything else you copy.
